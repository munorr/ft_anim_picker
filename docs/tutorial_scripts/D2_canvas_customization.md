# D2 — Canvas Customization — Background Images, Grid, Axes

**Target:** 2–3 min
**One-line hook:** Canvas settings panel, opacity, dots

---

## Intro

The default canvas is a clean dark grid — perfectly functional. But a **background image** turns a picker from a grid of buttons into an intuitive visual map of the character. In this video we'll cover everything you can customize about the canvas itself.

## Opening canvas settings

With Edit Mode on, look to the edit panel on the right. Below the button properties there's a **Canvas Settings** section. Click to expand it.

## Background image

Click **Background Image → Browse** and pick any image file — a character silhouette, a rig schematic, a hand-drawn diagram. BGEN loads it onto the canvas behind your buttons.

The image has its own **opacity slider** so you can fade it back until it's a quiet reference and your buttons stay the visible focus. Usually 20 to 40 percent opacity hits the right balance.

You can also set **X and Y offset** to position the image, and scale it to fit the canvas area you're designing for.

## Grid, axes, and dots

Three toggles control the built-in canvas visuals:

- **Grid** — the faint square grid. Great for aligning buttons by eye while you're building. You can turn it off for final delivery.
- **Axes** — the horizontal and vertical centerlines. Essential for symmetric rigs so you can see where the character's centerline is.
- **Dots** — small reference dots at regular intervals. An alternative to the grid if you want something less busy.

Most pickers keep axes on and grid off once the layout is finished.

## Per-tab canvas settings

One thing to know: **each tab has its own canvas settings**. Your Face tab can have a head reference image, your Body tab a full-body silhouette, and your Hands tab a close-up — all independent.

## A practical workflow

For a character rig, I usually:
1. Screenshot the rig in T-pose from the front
2. Drop that image into the Body tab's background at 30% opacity
3. Place buttons directly over the bones they control
4. Animators see the button and the body part at the same time — no guessing
